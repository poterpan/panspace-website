## Background & problem

At NTUT, timetables, grades, campus announcements and course registration live in separate university systems, each with its own login and none of them built for a phone. Finding out which room your next class is in means opening a browser, logging in and digging for the right page.

I wanted an app that answers that the moment you open it: log in once, and your timetable, grades and announcements are there, with a home-screen widget showing the next class.

## My role

Everything, solo: product decisions, the iOS app, the website, the course planner, the data pipeline, the backend services, deployment and monitoring.

## Approach & technical decisions

**The app** is SwiftUI with SwiftData for local storage, so the timetable works offline. Sign-in goes through the university's SSO flow, and the password stays in the iOS Keychain; it never touches my servers. The widgets share data with the app through an App Group and decide from the current time whether to show the class in progress or the next one.

**The course planner** at [course.ntutbox.com](https://course.ntutbox.com) only plans. It needs no login and never talks to the university's registration system on a student's behalf. A Python crawler runs on GitHub Actions: course listings and enrolment daily, syllabi and room schedules weekly, published as JSON to Cloudflare R2. The site is a static Next.js export with client-side full-text search. A finished plan opens in the app through a Universal Link, and the student confirms it there before anything is submitted. The planner is [open source](https://github.com/poterpan/ntutbox-course).

**The other services** run on Cloudflare: a [QR check-in system](https://github.com/poterpan/ntutbox-checkin) (Next.js, D1, KV, with QR codes that rotate every 30 seconds and one-time nonces against replays), a [timetable template API](https://github.com/poterpan/ntutbox-template-api) so students at other universities can import their period schedules, and a status page that monitors the university's systems. When the app misbehaves, the status page shows whether the university's side is down.
